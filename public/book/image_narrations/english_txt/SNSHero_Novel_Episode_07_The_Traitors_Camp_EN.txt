Episode 07: The Traitors' Pavilion

As the campfire smoke coiling across the vast plains of the Wildlands began to wane, the northern night sky gathered heavy charcoal snow-clouds as though preparing to unleash a merciless blizzard. The leather eaves of the yurts fluttered softly against the biting gale, and around the frigid troughs, iron horse-bells chimed with dull, muted clangs. Nomad families appeased the biting frost with strips of dry salted mutton and barley broth steeped in yak butter before extinguishing their tallow lamps for the night. On this final evening before venturing south, Kadan—now an eighteen-year-old wanderer forged by the tundra—stealthily crossed the craggy basalt ridges alongside the hermit Malfurion, hunting the sinister mana trail of Medusa Gorgo.

Gorgo had nestled into an abandoned cobalt quarry barely half a league from Chieftain Fenrir's vanguard camp. Inside the subterranean hollow, the stench of acrid sulfur and charred leather hung thick in the air. Upon the rock wall, she carved forbidden runes of the Nine Shadow Codex stolen from master Aeolus, honing her maddened sorcery. Each time her black-iron chain whip cracked against the stone, the slumbering mana within the bedrock withered away, crumbling into pale flakes of crystalline salt.

Just as Malfurion and Kadan held their breath in the shadows of a fissure, eight riders enveloped in black wool cloaks dismounted before the entrance. Astonishingly, the leading warlord was Bargast, sworn blood-brother to Fenrir and second-in-command of the entire tribal confederacy. Even more alarming was the crimson livery of the figure emerging from the gloom to greet him: an envoy bearing the gold-stitched flame crest of Lucian, prime minister of the Red Glass Court.

The envoy lowered his tone, proffering a wax-sealed parchment.

"The appointed strike falls upon tomorrow night. Paralyzing venom will be introduced into Fenrir's banquet horn. The instant Fenrir collapses, rally the clan lances beneath the banners of the Shadow Realm."

Bargast's hoarse rasp echoed along the damp stone.

"Fenrir's fall will ignite fierce outrage among the elders. When will the royal host reach our borders?"

The crimson envoy sneered coldly, tossing a heavy sack of gold coins.

"Ignis, son of Balian, marches north at the head of the Court's White Flame Order. You shall be crowned paramount warlord of the northern reaches, so banish your misgivings."

Hearing the names of Balian and Ignis fall from the emissary's lips sent a jolt through Kadan's chest. Delay was unthinkable. Following Malfurion's silent gesture, Kadan slipped away from the crag, turning their mounts toward Fenrir's war yurt.

Yet when they arrived at the council tent, Kadan was met not by trust, but by an unyielding barrier of suspicion. Seated upon the great white-wolf throne, Fenrir listened to the breathless warning of the youthful wanderer, but thirty winters of brotherhood blinded him to betrayal. Frost clung to the chieftain's iron-gray beard, and deep scars carved across his brow bore witness to dozens of harsh winters survived in blood and honor.

"Bargast has bled beside me for three decades hunting northern frost-beasts! Would you have me condemn my dearest brother upon the loose tongue of a wandering stripling?"

Chieftains muttered in agreement around the hearth. Worse still, Bargast had anticipated the blow, preemptively branding Malfurion as a southern infiltrator and binding him in heavy iron fetters.

Bargast strode into the pavilion, resting a gauntleted hand upon his bloody pommel. His eyes glinted like a hungry timber wolf, a mocking sneer curving his lips.

"Lord brother, I witnessed this vagrant elder and his young pup conspiring in secret with court delegates within the old cobalt mine! Slay these disruptors of northern unity at once!"

Bound in chains, Malfurion offered not a word of self-defense, gazing steadily at Kadan. It was a silent trial—an injunction to rely not upon borrowed authority or blind wrath, but upon senses and wits honed through discipline to lay bare the truth.

The conclusive thread emerged in the dead of night. Slipping beneath the baggage wagons, Titania pried loose shimmering shards of red volcanic glass from the saddle lining of a slain courier, scattering them across Fenrir's oak table. Such tempered glass was manufactured solely by the elite cavalry of the southern capital. Fenrir's calloused hands trembled, his weathered countenance turning rigid as frozen lake ice. At that very moment, Bargast entered the tent unescorted, bearing the wolf-fang amulet they had split between them in their youth.

"Brother, it is enough to graze within the borders set by our ancestors. Why force thirty clans into a single realm and invite the wrath of the throne? Take back this fang and surrender with honor."

Fenrir took the fang, pressing it deep into the frosty soil beneath his boots. His voice fell lower and heavier than the midnight gales.

"I seek to end the madness of children slaughtering one another over the same frozen well year after year. If tradition serves only to swell the count of our graves, I shall shatter it with my own hands."

At dawn, Bargast's rebels and imperial rune-knights stormed the perimeter hills. Common arrows shattered uselessly against the heavy plate of the vanguard, and the mud at the slope's foot echoed with screams. Blending the defensive arts of Master Aleksandar with Malfurion's star-breathing, Kadan held the breach. Tilting his iron shield to deflect charging lances into empty air, Kadan disarmed Bargast's ferocious son, Ursak, sending his broadsword spinning into the frost.

As allied braves raised their spears to claim the young captain's head, Kadan reversed his silver dagger and barred their path with his shield.

"Slaying a helpless captive breeds only terror, not victory. Cleanse his wounds and let him walk."

Ursak spat blood and muddy slush, glaring up with venomous fury.

"Save your false pity, boy! When my father sweeps this hill, your severed skull shall crown a watchpost!"

Without replying, Kadan retrieved a strip of clean linen, binding the gashed brow of the fallen youth before guiding him safely down the slope.

"Do not waste your life in service of your father's vain greed. Live, and witness the truth."

Witnessing such unexpected mercy and unbending resolve, the young rebel levies hesitated, lowering their spear-points across the front.

Three days later, Fenrir launched a decisive flanking assault, breaking Bargast's garrison. The ringleaders were condemned, yet Fenrir granted Bargast a cup of sweetened poison, preserving his final dignity as a warrior. While victory horns resounded over the steppes, Fenrir's eyes remained dark with sorrow. Beckoning Kadan to the armory tent, he offered chests of bullion and a greatbow carved of ancient dragon bone.

"Name your recompense, young warrior. I would carve you half the northern steppes."

Kadan shook his head, replying softly.

"Only ensure that the fallen conscripts from Bargast's levies are inscribed upon the ancestral rolls. True peace comes only when equal rest is granted to all who shed their blood."

Taking Kadan's hand, Fenrir proclaimed his betrothal to Titania before the gathered chiefs.

"I place my greatest treasure into your care. Become the lion of the steppes and lead our people!"

The throng roared in acclaim, but Titania slipped past the bonfires to stand quietly by the horse stalls. The north wind brushed her cheeks with frosty chill, yet her eyes shone with pristine clarity. As Kadan approached, she reached out, tightening the crimson silk ribbon bound to his dagger's hilt.

"I am neither prize nor chattel to be gifted, Kadan. When you return from the southern courts, ask me then whether I choose you."

Within her unyielding gaze, Kadan beheld the true, unbroken soul of the northern frontier.

"I shall remember, Titania. I swear to return alive so that your choice may never bring regret."

Beneath the vault of the midnight sky, stars drew shimmering silver paths behind their shoulders—an overture to the epic trials awaiting a wanderer destined for the southern realm.
