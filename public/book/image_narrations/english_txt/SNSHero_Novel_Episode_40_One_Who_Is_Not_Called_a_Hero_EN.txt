Episode 40: One Who Is Not Called a Hero

A month following the final battle, the imperial capital of Lighthaven thrummed with peaceful vitality. The charred wharves of Sevenbridge Port had been rebuilt through the labor of dwarf artisans and fishermen, while flower ships laden with grain and medicinal herbs lined the docks. In the cathedral square, the aged Emperor and the restored Senate convened a grand festival to honor the savior of the realm.

The Emperor stood from his gilded throne, unrolling an imperial decree. His voice boomed across the assembled thousands.

"People of the continent! To Kadan, disciple of Aleksandar who vanquished the Nine Shadows, I bestow the title of Grand Marshal and the rank of Duke! And to Princess Cilia of Whispering Wind Island, I offer the crown of Empress and Regent of the Eastern League!"

Cheers erupted across the plaza, the crowd shouting their names into the sky. Supreme power and boundless wealth lay before their feet.

Yet Kadan and Cilia stood before the dais clad not in velvet robes, but in simple traveler's furs and rough linen.

Stepping forward, Kadan bowed with deep courtesy, declining the golden coronet.

"I thank Your Majesty for this generous grace. Yet I can accept neither title nor dukedom. I am no hero; I am merely a wanderer who raised a shield to carry the warmth of my fallen master and Ignis to those who shiver in the cold. My place is not in marble halls, but upon the fertile earth of my homeland."

Cilia placed her jade staff upon the stone steps, offering a serene smile.

"Nor can I accept an imperial crown. The wind wears no coronet, nor can it be confined within palace corridors. My home is upon the plains where the people dwell."

Kadan touched the worn rim of his shield, his voice steady and calm.

"A shield is forged not to defend a throne, but to clasp the hands of shivering children. My place remains beside the hearth of my northern home."

Moved to tears by their humility, the citizens fell silent, bowing their heads in reverence as the two youths departed the dais.

That evening, away from the grand banquet, Kadan and Cilia shared ale beside the hearth of a humble tavern with King Fenrir, Rondol, and their comrades.

Fenrir clinked his timber tankard against Kadan's, laughing heartily.

"Hahaha! Men wage war their entire lives for crowns and provinces, yet you two cast them aside without a backward glance! You possess a stouter heart than Aleksandar ever did, Kadan."

Rondol stroked his wooden leg, emotion softening his rugged face.

"The flower ships of Whispering Wind Island are trade vessels now. Whenever you miss the ocean breezes, return to our shores. We remain brothers and allies for all time."

Kadan touched the Silver Dagger and the carved wooden horse toy of Ignis resting in his pocket.

"I shall never forget your kindness. A warm fire shall always await you in our northern cabin."

At dawn, Kadan and Cilia quietly departed Lighthaven's northern gate. There were no trumpet fanfares or armored escorts. On Kadan's back rested his scarred iron shield, while Cilia held a simple reed flute.

They walked along the winding dirt road toward the northern Elwin fief. Green shoots of winter barley poked through the thawed earth, and a warm spring breeze ruffled their hair. Cilia plucked a blue violet from the roadside, tucking it into the strap of Kadan's shield.

"We kept our promise, Kadan. Spring has truly arrived."

Kadan took her hand, his smile gentle and untroubled.

"Yes, Cilia. The winter was long, but spring never lost its way."

Upon the quiet hill of the Elwin fief, warm sunshine bathed the side-by-side graves of Master Aleksandar and Uncle Balian. Wildflowers bloomed in abundance across the grassy mound. Kneeling together, Kadan and Cilia poured fresh spring water upon the stone markers. Between the two graves, they gently buried Ignis's small wooden horse toy beneath the rich soil.

Kadan closed his eyes, whispering softly to the wind.

"Master, father... and Ignis. We brought spring to this land as promised. Here we shall till the soil and wash the feet of weary travelers."

The spring breeze rustled the grass, as if ancient heroes were resting gentle hands upon their shoulders.

From the chimney of a small log cabin at the foot of the hill, white woodsmoke drifted into the azure sky. The cabin door stood wide open, and beside the hearth rested an empty chair for any traveler seeking shelter from the cold.

The age of heroes had ended, but the quiet, beautiful story of living mortals was only just beginning. The legend of the Snow Hero would live on, not in frozen histories, but in the enduring warmth shared beside the hearths of the people.
