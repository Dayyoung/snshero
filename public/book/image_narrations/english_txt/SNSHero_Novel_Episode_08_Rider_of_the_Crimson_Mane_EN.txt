Episode 08: Rider of the Crimson Mane

After half a day's hard riding south from the frost-locked tundras of the Wildlands, the landscape gradually transformed from stony wastes into the misty border citadel of Pegasus Valley. Beyond the low parapets of rough-hewn rubble, the creaking timber of freight wagons mingled with the coarse shouts of mercenary bands arriving from every quadrant of the realm. Venders along the muddy thoroughfares tended iron pots of boiling turnips and roasted potatoes, the pungent aroma of woodsmoke and damp straw drifting upon the brisk autumn air. Under tavern eaves, coarse gamblers cursed violently over lost dice games. Eighteen-year-old Kadan, having parted ways with the hermit Malfurion to journey south alone, meditated upon the tenets of Master Aleksandar as he found an unoccupied wooden bench at the edge of the market stalls.

In a broad stockade corral at the edge of the marketplace, a horse auction was in full swing. Brandishing leather whips, the traders waved red pennants and bellowed to the crowd, yet the spectators stood ten paces back in murmuring apprehension. In the center of the enclosure stood an enormous wild stallion, its crimson mane billowing like an autumn cascade across sleek, obsidian-black flanks. It was the legendary wild stallion 'Crimson Mane.' Deemed hopelessly feral and branded a wild beast by royal cavalry examiners, the magnificent mount faced imminent slaughter at the knacker's block. Across its muscular chest ran crisscrossed scars from heavy whips and rusted chains.

The chief auctioneer cracked his iron chain against the dirt.

"Three silver pieces! Any gallant soul willing to claim this mad demon for three silver coins? Stand clear unless you fancy having your skulls caved in!"

Maddened by the clinking chains, Crimson Mane reared high, its iron-shod hooves battering the timber railing. Heavy oak palisades cracked with violent splinters. As the onlookers scattered in panic, Kadan stepped calmly through the fractured gate, bearing no weapons in his hands.

The auctioneer turned pale, shrieking in horror.

"Boy! Are you possessed? Back away before you are trampled into red paste!"

Ignoring the cries, Kadan lowered his gaze, harmonizing his respiration with the ragged snorts of the wild mount. When the stallion charged with thundering fury, Kadan neither fled nor drew steel. Moving fluidly along the beast's flank via the star-breathing discipline, he swept past the shoulder and laid a gentle, steady palm upon the trembling crest of its neck. Sensing the deep rhythm of the earth and an unshakable heartbeat devoid of malice, the beast shuddered. Kadan gently stroked the calloused skin where iron bits had torn the horse's muzzle.

"No chain shall bind you in pain again. If you wish to run, the boundless plains are yours."

As the setting sun cast long amber shadows over the dust, Crimson Mane lowered its proud head, resting its velvet muzzle softly against Kadan's leather vambrace. Leaving behind the stunned cheers of the crowd, Kadan led the stallion by its halter into the bustling tavern lane in search of a warm meal.

At the entrance to the lane, a commotion broke out. A slender girl enveloped in a frayed wool mantle was being violently shoved against a timber counter by a corpulent grain merchant. It was Cilia, the apprentice wind mage.

The merchant brandished an ash truncheon, his face purple with rage.

"Thieving gutter-rat! You crept behind my grain sacks to steal flour! I'll hand you over to the garrison dungeons!"

Soot-smudged cheeks held high, Cilia retorted with razor-sharp defiance.

"Cease your mindless squawking! Look at the flour dust near the cellar hatch. There are no tiny straw-sandal tracks—only the broad wooden clogs of the tavern bouncer across the street. If the gods gave you eyes, try employing your brain!"

As the enraged merchant raised his truncheon to strike, Kadan stepped between them, nudging the weapon aside with the rim of his iron shield.

"The girl speaks the truth, and the evidence is clear. Lower your stick."

Flipping a silver coin onto the counter to quiet the dispute, Kadan guided the famished girl to a nearby soup stall. He ordered two steaming bowls of beef-bone broth with turnips and a warm loaf of sesame bread.

When the soup arrived, Cilia surreptitiously dipped a silver hairpin into the broth to test for narcotics. Noticing her wariness, Kadan took up his wooden spoon and swallowed a generous draught first. Cilia arched a skeptical brow.

"You place blind trust in strangers far too easily. What if I am a cunning cutpurse waiting to pick your pockets?"

Kadan tore the warm sesame loaf in half, offering the larger portion.

"Anyone willing to endure a truncheon blow to prove the truth is genuinely starving. Eat first."

Cilia stared at him in quiet astonishment, then took a sip of the savory broth, her shoulders softening in the steam.

"...My name is Cilia. I was making my way toward the Grand Academy in the capital. And who might you be?"

"I am Kadan of the Wildlands. I seek High Commander Ignis of the White Flame Order."

At the mention of Ignis, Cilia's gaze frosted over instantly. She set her spoon down, murmuring bitterly.

"Ignis... So you seek the gilded puppet of the Flame Court. You will only find yourself ensnared in the venal machinations of the high lords. You would do well to tread lightly."

As they departed the shadowed alleyways after nightfall, a dozen horse thieves lying in wait barred their path, naked steel gleaming in the moonlight. The bandit leader grinned malevolently.

"Hand over the crimson stallion and your purse, and you might crawl away with your hides! That warhorse will keep our crew in luxury for a year!"

Before Kadan could brace his shield, Cilia gave a crisp snap of her fingers.

"Muscular brawn alone is clumsy in tight corridors. Watch the wind's eye."

With a swift incantation, a miniature whirlwind tore through the lane, collapsing the striped canvas awnings over the raiders. Trapped beneath heavy tarpaulins, the thieves stumbled blindly while rolling barrels of winter apples took out their knees. The brigands collapsed in a tangled heap of bruised limbs and howling curses.

Kadan let out a genuine chuckle of admiration.

"You are certainly no ordinary beggar girl."

Cilia brushed her cloak with a satisfied smirk.

"Naturally. So pay heed to my counsel hereafter."

At that instant, violent detonations and terrified screams erupted from the central duel platform. The wandering knight Balbo was battling desperately to protect his foster daughter, Eliana. His adversary was none other than High Commander Ignis, inspecting the frontier with an escort of royal knights.

With an immaculate sweep of his blade, Ignis unleashed arcs of pure white fire, ruthlessly incinerating Eliana's protective briar wards. The fury of the flames was unbridled, scorching the stone floor in blackened rings of heat. As Balbo collapsed upon the flagstones coughing crimson, Ignis casually kicked Eliana's torn leather shoe into the mud below with cold contempt.

While the spectators cowered in silence, Kadan strode steadily up the timber stairs. Retrieving the muddy shoe, he gently slipped it onto Eliana's foot, then tightened his grip upon the silver dagger with its crimson ribbon, staring directly into Ignis's eyes.

"If you have won, you have nothing left to prove. Why humiliate the fallen?"

Ignis looked upon the silver dagger and the battered iron shield in the stranger's hands. Recognizing the ancient runic engraving of the House of Aleksandar on the pommel, an inexplicable surge of turbulent fury and electric recognition pierced Ignis's heart.

Leveling his blade with icy malice, Ignis sneered.

"A vagrant dares preach righteousness to me? Let us see if that tarnished dagger can withstand my flame!"

Thus began the fateful, fiery reckoning between the two destined youths, as cold autumn mists gathered over the battlements to shroud their drawn blades.
